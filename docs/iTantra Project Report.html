iTantra · PS 26173Abstract
SECTION 01

Abstract

A disaster radio link carries a few hundred bits per second. Human speech, as audio, needs several thousand. iTantra closes that gap by transmitting meaning instead of waveform.

In alert and distress scenarios, voice is the only interface that includes everyone — literate or not, gloved or not, in any of ten Indian scripts. But the links that survive when infrastructure fails, LoRa and HF and narrowband satellite, cannot carry a voice call. This is not an engineering gap awaiting a better codec; it is a bound. Audio codecs compress the shape of a pressure wave, and a waveform detailed enough to be understood has an irreducible size.

iTantra removes the waveform from the link entirely. An Android application recognises speech on the sending handset, transmits the resulting text as a few dozen bytes, and re-synthesises speech on the receiving handset. Both operators only ever speak and listen; neither reads nor types. Ten Indian languages run entirely on-device, with no cloud, no SIM, no network permission and no proprietary SDK.

iTantra · PS 26173The bandwidth bound
SECTION 02

The problem, to scale

Three numbers decide the whole project, and they are far enough apart that a linear axis cannot show them together.

To reproduce a voice with enough fidelity that words remain distinguishable, a microphone samples air pressure sixteen thousand times a second at sixteen bits — a quarter of a million bits for every second of speech. Opus, the best open-source speech codec available, carries an intelligible voice at around six thousand bits per second. A narrowband disaster link offers roughly three hundred.

Why the interface must still be voice

If the payload is text, why not let people type it? Because the problem statement's own justification is inclusion. In a disaster the person holding critical information may be a fisherman, a farmer or a village elder; a soldier at altitude in gloves; a rescuer on a rope. Speaking a sentence takes three seconds. Typing it in Devanagari, in the dark, with cold hands, takes forty — if it happens at all.

So the interface is voice, because voice is inclusive. The transmission is text, because text fits. iTantra is the machine between the two.

SECTION 03

Compressing meaning, not waveform

The system degrades gracefully through three levels, choosing the most aggressive one the situation allows.

LevelMechanism3 s Hindi sentenceOn a 300 bps link
—Raw PCM, 16 kHz 16-bit mono96 000 B43 min
—Opus at 6 kbps2 250 B60 s
L1Semantic transcoding — speech to UTF-8 text99 B2.6 s
L2Script packing — one byte per Indic codepoint32 B1.2 s
L3Template code — one byte per canned sentence1 B0.3 s

Level 1 · Semantic transcoding

Speech becomes text. This is where the enormous ratio comes from — roughly 770 to 1 before any further work. Everything below is refinement.

Level 2 · Script packing

UTF-8 spends three bytes per character on Devanagari, Tamil, Kannada and the rest, because it must be able to represent every script at once. But the frame header already declares the language, so the receiver knows which 128-codepoint Unicode block applies. Subtracting the block base yields a single byte per character. The transformation is lossless, table-driven, costs microseconds, and is available only because we control both endpoints.

Real messages are not pure Indic script: they carry Latin digits, punctuation, and the zero-width joiners that shape conjuncts. An escape sequence — 0x1B followed by a 24-bit codepoint — covers anything outside the declared block, at four bytes for a character that would otherwise be silently lost.

Level 3 · Template codes

In an emergency, vocabulary is small and predictable. A shared table maps common operational sentences to single-byte identifiers. After recognition the text is fuzzy-matched against that table; on a confident match the system sends the identifier and the receiver renders the full sentence.

A consequence worth noticing

Because every handset holds the template table in all ten languages, a template-coded message is spoken in whichever language the receiver has selected. A Hindi speaker's alert reaches a Tamil speaker in Tamil — with no translation model, no extra download and no extra latency. Cross-language operation falls out of the compression scheme. It applies to template traffic only, and the report claims nothing wider.

What is discarded, and what compensates

LostCompensation
Speaker identitySender byte in the header, display name in the interface, and a distinct synthesis voice per node
Emotion and urgencyExplicit priority type; alert traffic routed to the alarm stream with a faster, harder delivery profile

The boundary that matters

core-proto carries no Android dependency at all. The highest-risk code in the project — the frame codec — is therefore unit-tested and fuzzed on a laptop in seconds rather than on a handset in minutes, and the entire transport layer can be built and proven before a single model exists.

Threading

Audio work never touches the interface thread and the recogniser never blocks capture. Four threads with bounded queues between them: capture at urgent-audio priority, which must not allocate; inference; link; and synthesis. The engine lives in a foreground service, because the normal operating state for a radio is screen-off.

ThreadDutyConstraint
Capture20 ms blocks, energy gate, VADZero allocation, no logging
InferenceEncoder and decoder, partial and final hypothesesMay lag transiently; the queue absorbs it
LinkBlocking socket I/O, framing, retries, heartbeatThe only place blocking I/O is expected
SynthesisChunked generation feeding AudioTrackMust stay ahead of the playback clock

Ten bytes of header and two of trailer buy channels, priority, relay, integrity and authentication. The payload is what remains.

Reporting the honest ratio

2 182× is the unauthenticated frame against raw PCM. In any deployment worth defending the payload is sealed, and the figure is 1 600×. Against the Opus floor — the comparison a technical reviewer will actually ask for — it is 43×. All four ratios are carried in the evaluation scorecard rather than only the largest.

Integrity and authenticity

The problem statement asks for none of this. It is included because the system wakes a locked, silenced handset and announces at maximum volume on command; a device with that capability and no authentication is a liability rather than a deployable system.

  • AES-256-GCM with a pre-shared key held by every paired unit. The entire header is associated data, so the sender identity cannot be altered without invalidating the tag.
  • Derived nonce — EPOCH ‖ SRC ‖ SEQ, never transmitted, saving twelve bytes per frame.
  • Sliding replay window, 64 entries per sender, so a recorded alert cannot be replayed.
  • Optical key transfer only. The key crosses by QR code and never touches the radio medium, which is what makes a pre-shared key meaningfully pre-shared.

iTantra · PS 26173Speech engines
SECTION 06

Recognition and synthesis

Three-tier detection

Running the acoustic model continuously would exhaust the battery in about two hours and fail the efficiency criterion outright. Detection is tiered, so the expensive stage runs only when the cheap stages agree that a human is speaking.

TierMechanismCost / 20 msPurpose
0Energy gate against an adaptive noise floor~3 µsRejects silence — most of elapsed time
1Silero VAD, 1.8 MB~300 µsSeparates speech from wind, engines, doors
2Conformer acoustic model~4 msRecognition. Runs only when tier 1 agrees

Endpointing

The problem statement asks for activation "after detecting pauses and stoppages". A state machine finalises on 400 ms of trailing silence in phone mode, or 150 ms after key release in push-to-talk. A 250 ms leading pad is retained so the first phoneme is never clipped, and utterances are bounded at 8 s so one speaker cannot monopolise a half-duplex channel.

The silence window is the single largest latency term — and it is a design choice, not a computational cost. Push-to-talk is measurably faster precisely because releasing the key replaces it.

Contextual biasing

Decoding scores are boosted for a supplied phrase list. In a distress context the critical vocabulary is small and known in advance: मदद, घायल, आग, निकासी, unit callsigns, sector numbers, place names.

This measurably reduces error on precisely the words whose misrecognition would be most costly, and requires no retraining. Negation terms are weighted heavily, because a recognition error that turns "do not evacuate" into "now evacuate" inverts meaning and is the most dangerous failure the system can produce.

Quantisation

Models are trained in 32-bit floating point, which is unnecessary for inference. Dynamic quantisation rewrites the weights as 8-bit integers, exploiting the fact that ARM cores execute integer arithmetic considerably faster than float.

PrecisionSizeRelative speedWER impactVerdict
float32, as trained~120 MB1.0×baselineUnshippable
float16~60 MB1.3×≈ 0Intermediate
int8 dynamic~32 MB2.5×+1 % rel.Shipped

Four times smaller and two and a half times faster for roughly one per cent relative degradation. This single transformation is what makes on-device speech viable on an entry-tier handset.

Synthesis

Two of the four synthesis stages are ordinary software engineering rather than machine learning, and they account for most of the perceived quality. Text normalisation decides that 112 is dialled digit-wise while 5 km is a quantity — a rule, not a model, and the difference between a synthesiser that sounds professional and one that sounds broken. Chunked playout splits the sentence at clause boundaries and begins playing the first chunk while later ones are still generating, cutting time-to-first-audio from roughly 700 ms to 180 ms for the same total work.

Read the last two rows together. On the link that matters operationally — the one that works when infrastructure does not — audio is impossible and iTantra is comfortable. That gap is the product.

The deployment path

Bluetooth RFCOMM is a virtual serial cable, and it is the same profile an ESP32 or a radio modem exposes. The code that talks to another phone is the code that talks to a radio. Approximately ₹1 500 of hardware and two hundred lines of firmware convert a phone-to-phone demonstration into a deployable system.

iTantra · PS 26173Evaluation
SECTION 08

Evaluation methodology

Eighty per cent of the assessment is measurable, so the application is instrumented from the first commit and prints its own scorecard.

The stated criteria are Accuracy 40 %, Latency 20 % and Efficiency 20 %. Between them they force a real harness over held-out data under noise, instrumentation shipped inside the application rather than a stopwatch, and quantisation behind a tiered wake-up path.

Measurement conditions

Figures are reportable only from the entry-tier target handset, in a release build, after a thirty-minute thermal soak, over at least a hundred utterances, as median and 95th percentile. Entry-tier handsets throttle after about ten minutes of continuous inference and real-time factor can double, so sustained numbers are both more honest and more defensible than peak ones.

Accuracy — reported at four noise levels

Word error rate is measured on held-out benchmark data — IndicSUPERB, Kathbath, IndicVoices, FLEURS — never on self-recorded samples. Clean-room figures do not represent a flood embankment.

ConditionTarget WERComment
Clean, read speech< 12 %Reference figure
+20 dB SNR< 15 %Quiet room, realistic
+10 dB SNR< 22 %Vehicle, crowd

The last row is the important one: overall word error rate is a poor proxy for usefulness, and what matters is whether मदद, आग, sector numbers and callsigns survive. It is reported with and without biasing in the same table, so the delta is attributable to a deliberate engineering decision.

Open-source compliance

The problem statement prohibits proprietary, closed-source and commercial voice-activation SDKs. No such component appears anywhere in the system; every model executes on the device and no network request is made at runtime. The claim is enforced by the platform rather than asserted by us — the shipped manifest declares no INTERNET permission.

Status, plan and conclusion

Where the project stands

The system is fully specified — roughly thirty-seven thousand words covering requirements traceability, architecture, a byte-level wire protocol, both speech engines, transport, security, evaluation methodology, a risk register and the complete interface design. Implementation has not begun; the task breakdown contains 207 items across eight weeks.

WeekObjectiveGate
1Capture and recogniseHindi speech produces text on the target handset, offline
2TransportTyped text crosses two phones; decoder survives fuzzing
3Close the loopSpeech in on A, speech out on B
4Real modelsFive languages; the harness produces a scorecard
5Modes and alertsAn alert wakes a locked, silenced handset
6Latency and reachEncryption, four transports, first thermal soak
7CoverageTen languages; field and relay soaks clean
8EvidenceFull scorecard; three clean rehearsals
The governing rule

Week three is the hinge. A working loop with mediocre models can be improved under any amount of time pressure; excellent models with no loop cannot be demonstrated at all. Model quality is an upgrade path, never a prerequisite.

Conclusion

iTantra is not a speech-recognition demonstration with a Bluetooth link attached. It is a communications system that uses speech models as its codec. That framing matches the problem statement's own language — neural transceiver radio access for low bitrate links — and it is the framing under which every decision in this report is coherent rather than arbitrary.

The measure of success is narrow and testable: two handsets in aeroplane mode, ten languages, sub-second delivery, an installer under thirty megabytes, idle CPU below two per cent verified by trace rather than assertion, word error rate reported per language and per noise condition, alerts that wake a locked and silenced handset — and the same bytes crossing a real radio link.
